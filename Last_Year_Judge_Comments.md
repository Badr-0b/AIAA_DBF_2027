Judges Comments:
Overall Summary

You have a good of the mechanics of the contest. I'm worried that you don't understand the nuances of the missions and missed an opportunity to explain your design given you still had room on page 6 to fill. Your organization and schedule explanation was adequate. I was hoping for more detail on costs for the payloads. Your discussion for the rest of the sections could have been more robust and lengthy. The table are concise but you miss opportunities to explain the details. You don't need to repeat content that was in the request for proposal. I'm sure you can get an aircraft to fly but I don't have confidence that you understand how well you will score.
No income, cost, banner length, or lap estimate. This should have been proofread. Lots of grammar issues. Need more discussion of sensitivity studies and plans for M2 and M3.
The proposal hit the major points, but often lacked the necessary depth. For example, it is not clear how design requirement drove the design. This would have included, for example, a cost analysis for Mission 2
What worked: Clear, workable configuration with sensible trades (Clark-Y wing; conventional tail; tricycle gear) and a credible, phased build & test plan with checklists and dates. The trade tables (airfoil/gear/tail) and isometric model show good early discipline. What needs improvement: 1. Mission Math is missing. You need concrete numbers for Mission 2 (passenger/cargo mix, lap time target, EF from total Wh, per-lap income/cost, net income) and for Mission 3 (banner length, projected laps, RAC, and the resulting score metric). 2. No autopilots/flight controllers are allowed. Your “stability and programming” description mentions algorithms; keep to pilot-in-the-loop with optional rate gyros only, and state that clearly. 3. Magnets are not an acceptable sole latching method for hatches; add a positive mechanical latch (spring pin, screw, cotter, etc.) and keep magnets only as secondary retention. 4. With WS = 4.8 ft, RAC ≈ 0.99. Put this number in the summary and propagate it into your Mission 3 score estimate. 5. Show how the optimum # passengers vs. # cargo vs. # laps changes with lap time and EF; then pick a specific plan (e.g., “6 ducks + 2 pucks × 4 laps at 48 s/lap, EF = 0.72”) and explain why that maximizes net income for your airplane. 6. Add explicit line items for rubber ducks and hockey pucks (test articles), packaging/crating, spare props, adhesives/paint, and consumables. Tie the funding line to a confirmed amount/PO from the faculty budget and include contingency. 7. Time your full GM workflow end-to-end with the final latching scheme; design payload fixtures for single-handed rapid loading and clearly state how the banner is externally stowed and deployed. You have a credible airplane concept and a thoughtful manufacturing/test plan. The biggest gap is turning the concept into a scoring plan with real numbers that prove rule compliance and maximize Missions 2 & 3.
Thank you for your submission!"
